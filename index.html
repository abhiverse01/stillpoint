The loud default, and the alternative

Most progress bars are designed like alarms. stillpoint is designed like a held breath.

Hard edges, primary colors, a countdown that begs to be watched.

A gradient with a soft leading edge, paced by an easing curve instead of your CPU.

Three ways to sit with a wait

Determinate work, indeterminate waits, and a quiet mode for people who'd rather not watch at all.

// a loop with a known length

from stillpoint import stillpoint for item in stillpoint(range(100), label="training"): train_step(item)

// a wait with no known total

with stillpoint.breathe(label="waiting for response"): slow_network_call()

// for people who find bars distracting

for item in stillpoint(data, mode="quiet"): process(item)

Design notes

  • 01Time-driven, not tick-driven. Frames redraw at a capped rate regardless of loop speed, so a tight inner loop never floods your terminal.
  • 02Numbers set with care. Counts are right-aligned to a fixed width so the line doesn't jitter as digits grow.
  • 03No fake progress. Indeterminate waits get a breathing glyph, not a bar creeping toward a number it can't back up.
  • 04Zero dependencies. Built on ANSI escape codes and the standard library — nothing else installs when stillpoint does.
